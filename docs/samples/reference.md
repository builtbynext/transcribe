# Highlighter App — Brain dump

**Monetization / Business Model**

When I think about starting this annotation and highlighter tool, I’m considering different possibilities for monetization and how it might work.

One idea is a freemium model, where I offer the basic highlighter functionality for free, and then charge for more advanced features like AI summaries or capabilities inspired by tools like GoodNotes. This would help me validate two things: first, whether highlighting itself is useful for people, and second, whether users actually want—and are willing to pay for—those premium features.

If I charge for the highlighter from the start, I might run into issues. The app could be buggy or not work well initially, and since I don’t want to overcomplicate things, I’d rather keep it free at the beginning. I also have other features in mind that I can build progressively, but right now I just want to get a useful MVP out there—something that solves my own problems and hopefully helps others too.

**Distribution**

In terms of distribution, I’m thinking about channels like Reddit—especially since platforms like reMarkable already have communities there. I could also explore places like Product Hunt later on. For now, those can come after the MVP is ready.

**Vision**

The vision for the product is still a bit unclear, but one idea is that regardless of which e-reader someone uses, they can take notes or highlights and have everything stored in a searchable way. This would help people better remember what they read. Personally, I find that I often forget what I’ve read after some time, which makes the effort feel wasted. Making this process easier and more effective is something I believe many readers struggle with. I want to solve this for myself and potentially for others too.

**Future Ideas for highlighter app**

All of this information could live in something like a wiki, where people can search, learn, and contribute. Over time, we could explore monetization options such as job postings—connecting people with help in areas like accounting, development, design, or go-to-market strategy. These are common needs across most businesses, so providing tools and connections could be valuable. There’s also potential for brand partnerships. Since this space is evolving so quickly, there are many opportunities to build something meaningful here.

1. **Vibe coding community**

I also like the idea of a community model, which could be a separate but related business. It would be a space where people share workflows, tools, and experiences. We could interview people, document their processes, and even turn it into a podcast-style format. With the rise of AI, many people are building apps they couldn’t before, but they face similar challenges—like incorporation, costs, and figuring out what setup makes sense.

1. **New Standard Design Studio**

The third area I’m exploring is web design. I’ve already decided on a name and now need to work on the mood board, create a landing page, and submit for partnership agreements. Once I’m fully onboarded, I can start taking on projects. Ideally, I want this to be a fully remote setup and automate as much as possible—especially in terms of finding businesses and figuring out how to help them.

1. **Vipassana meditation-related product**

The fourth idea I’m exploring is a something that makes it easier for people to get into a flow state quickly. This would likely start as a personal tool. I haven’t validated it with many people yet, but I’m curious about improving the experience, especially around meditation tracks. Many of them are copyrighted, so I’m wondering if AI could help create alternatives that are less distracting or more tailored.

**Summary**

At the beginning, I want to keep things simple and test a few hypotheses, like offering AEO (AI Engine Optimization) and SEO from the start. As I learn, I can offer these services at low cost or even free initially, so I can improve while helping businesses grow. I also want to automate processes as much as possible to reduce manual work. The goal is to make it as hands-free as possible—clients can manage things themselves after setup, or retain me for ongoing support.

**Potential business model for NewStandard.design**

Another angle here is creating templates and tools. This helps me practice, build faster, and get my work out there. Some of these could be monetized, while others could be free—similar to how Tailwind started with free components before introducing paid ones. I’m also open to contributing to open-source projects.

Overall, this feels exciting because I get to work on different things. I’m someone who enjoys variety, and this is something I couldn’t fully do in a corporate job where I had to focus on emails and structured tasks. Now that I have more flexibility, I can explore more freely. I’ve also realized that I enjoy building more than watching tutorials—I learn better by doing and figuring things out along the way.

**To recap**

To recap, the first idea is the highlighter tool for e-ink devices. The second is a community platform for builders. The third is a web/design studio focused on helping brands launch quickly with high-quality, high-trust outputs, while automating things like SEO and AEO.

That said, I’m not fully convinced the third idea will work yet, mainly because there are many competitors in that space. I may need to find a more unique angle.

Those are the main ideas I’m exploring for now.
